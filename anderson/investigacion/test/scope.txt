SCOPE DEFINITION - RESEARCH PROJECT
Session date: 2026-08-25
Type: Systematic review (PRISMA 2020) with quantitative synthesis (meta-regression),
potentially graduating to a "meta-analysis" label if the course/supervisor requires it.

================================================================================
1. TOPIC
================================================================================
The coding and software-engineering capability of LOCALLY-RUNNABLE large
language models (LLMs), and how that capability has evolved over time relative
to the frontier.

================================================================================
2. POPULATION (INCLUSION CRITERIA) - "Local model" definition
================================================================================
Feasibility-defined, self-hosted models. A locally-runnable model is one whose
Q4_K_M (GGUF) weights plus modest KV cache fit within consumer hardware, fully
resident (no SSD/CPU streaming), at usable speed.

- Primary budget:     16 GB VRAM at Q4_K_M   (mainstream tier, ~26% of consumers,
                      largest single tier per Steam Hardware Survey 2026)
- Upper envelope:     24 GB VRAM              (captures Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B and
                                              32B dense; ~5% power-user tier)
- Sensitivity band:   8 GB VRAM
- Apple Silicon:      unified memory up to ~48 GB admitted on equivalent terms
- Quantization floor: Q4_K_M (sub-Q4, e.g. Q2/Q3 or SSD-streamed large MoE, excluded)
- Speed floor:        >= 15-20 tokens/second fully resident on a representative
                      16-24 GB consumer GPU (or 24-48 GB Apple unified memory)

Models admitted (representative): Qwen2.5-Coder 7/14/32B, Qwen3 4-32B,
Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (24 GB), Llama 3.1/3.2 8B, Mistral 7B/8B/Small-24B,
Gemma 3 4B/12B/27B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill 7/14/32B, Phi-4 / Phi-4-mini.

Models EXCLUDED: Llama 3.3 70B, Llama 3.1 405B, Llama 4 Scout/Maverick,
Mistral Large 123B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-70B, GLM-4.5-Air, data-center-scale MoEs
(e.g. DeepSeek V3/R1 671B), any model requiring >24 GB VRAM / >48 GB unified
memory or SSD streaming to run.

================================================================================
3. RESEARCH QUESTION
================================================================================
FORMAL (PICOS-style):
  Among locally-runnable LLMs (consumer-hardware feasibility constraint),
  how has coding and software-engineering benchmark performance evolved
  across model release dates from 2023 to 2026, and how large is the remaining
  gap to the best closed proprietary models?

Components:
- CORE RQ (primary):  How has the coding/SWE ability of locally-runnable models
                      improved over time (by release date)?
- SECONDARY RQ:       How large is the current gap between the best local models
                      and the closed frontier?
- DISCUSSION ONLY:    "Is the future of AI in the hands of local models?" --
                      answered via the trend slope + feasibility analysis, not
                      as a primary outcome.

6. ARCHITECTURE (HOW THE TWO EVIDENCE STREAMS COMBINE)
================================================================================
Literature-first (classic SR-with-synthesis shape):
  1) PRISMA search produces the screened corpus of studies that evaluate
     >=1 feasible-local model on >=1 pre-specified benchmark.
  2) Extract model x benchmark x evaluation scores from each included study.
  3) Benchmark leaderboards SUPPLEMENT and VERIFY extractions:
     Epoch AI Benchmarking Hub CSV, LiveBench snapshots, SWE-bench Verified
     official leaderboard, EvalPlus leaderboard (LLM Stats, Artificial Analysis
     used as secondary/cross-check sources).
  4) Quantitative synthesis runs on the merged dataset.
Leaderboards/benchmark repos are documented as DATA SOURCES (gray literature
appendix), not screened as studies.

================================================================================
7. EVIDENCE POLICY
================================================================================
- Data points admissible from public benchmark data + peer-reviewed evaluations.
- Every row tagged with provenance:
    * source type
    * self-reported vs independently run
    * contamination-resistant benchmark or not
- Sensitivity analysis restricted to independently-verified scores only.
- No artificial cap on study count: inclusion levers are relevance filters;
  include every study that passes.

================================================================================
8. COMPARISON DESIGN (THE CENTRAL FIGURE)
================================================================================
Three-line design:
  1) Feasible-local set   (the population defined in section 2)
  2) All-open-weight, feasibility-blind  (incl. data-center-scale open models,
     e.g. DeepSeek giants -- the line Epoch/WeirdML already track)
  3) Closed proprietary frontier (best closed models per benchmark)

This decomposes the gap into:
  open-vs-closed (already studied)  +  FEASIBILITY PENALTY (our contribution).

Frontier anchor methodology: ENVELOPE-OF-BEST.
  At each date, frontier = the best closed-model score per benchmark;
  local gap = distance of the best feasible-local envelope to the closed
  frontier envelope, per benchmark per date. No composite black-box index.

Qwen treatment: NEUTRAL. No special handling; the best models win on the data.
If Qwen dominates, that is a result, not advocacy.

================================================================================
10. RISK-OF-BIAS TOOL
================================================================================
Tailored appraisal of each included evaluation on five items:
  1) Independent run vs self-reported
  2) Harness/version transparency
  3) Benchmark contamination-resistance
  4) Sample/compute adequacy
  5) Reporting completeness
Quality flags feed the sensitivity analyses.

================================================================================
11. SYNTHESIS MODEL
================================================================================
- Mixed-effects meta-regression on 0-100 normalized benchmark scores.
- Predictors: release date (the slope IS the headline: how fast local models
  are improving), log params, hardware band (8/16/24 GB), benchmark family.
- Random effects clustered by model and by benchmark.
- Gap metric: distance from best feasible-local envelope to closed-frontier
  envelope, per benchmark per date.
- Sensitivity analyses:
    * independently-verified scores only
    * contamination-resistant benchmarks only
    * each hardware band separately
